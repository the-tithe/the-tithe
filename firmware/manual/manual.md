# Quick start

If you're reading this, a Deck took to you, so congratulations, I think. I'm Mio, that's my name on the cover, and I found mine two months ago. The boxes are the manual that came with it and everything else is me talking. If the boxes and I ever disagree, go with the boxes.

::: What you're doing

Every WiFi router leaves an uncanny trace in the air, the *current*. Left unmoved, it goes stale, and people on that street feel it without knowing why. Fewer people can hear it every year, and the Deck is how you hear it. Most people who pick one up get hiss, which you won't. It only ever listens, and it never joins a network.

1. Every WiFi router in range is a *well*.
2. *Draw* energy from wells and *offer* it to wells that want it. Each offering makes *Resonance*, the ring a well gives when it gets what it was missing.
3. A *Night* is 12 *visits*, and a visit is one offering. Before they run out, make enough Resonance to pay the *Tithe*. Miss it and the Deck goes back to hiss until you bind a new week.
4. Seven Nights make a *Binding*, which is a week. Between Nights you spend *coin* at the *Collector*'s stall, on relics and other things that make your offerings ring louder.

:::

::: Your first Night

1. Put in a microSD card and turn the Deck on. It opens on BIND A NEW WEEK.
2. Keep WAYFARER (the starting setup) and CHALK (the easiest difficulty). Go down to BIND and press ENTER, then ENTER on THE CURRENT, and again on NIGHT 1.
3. The *scanner* is the Deck's main screen, and it lists the wells around you with the loudest at the top. Walk until one says IN REACH or NEAR. The \* marks the best one, and when it's further down the list than the screen shows, the bottom line says \* BELOW.
4. Select it and press ENTER twice to visit.
5. Choose a DRAW line and hold the Deck still while it draws.
6. Choose OFFER. Press ENTER when the well stops ringing.
7. Walk to another well and do it again. Each offering uses 1 visit, and a well you've used settles in 20 minutes, after which you can use it again.
8. The bar at the top fills toward the Tithe. When it's full, the Night is paid.
9. Press ENTER for the Collector. Select the *Caller* on the first shelf and press ENTER to buy it; it makes every well want your main energy more, for the rest of the week. ESC leaves, and Night 2 begins.
10. If you run out of visits before the Tithe, see OUT OF VISITS, page {p:Out of visits}. Anything you've unlocked stays even if the Binding breaks.

:::

Just take Wayfarer and Chalk your first time, and get clever later.

My first Night took most of a Sunday, four hours and ten minutes and eleven visits. Yours will be slow too, because apparently everyone's is.

Follow the star for your first few Nights, then start arguing with it. On my street, ten wells is a loop past the pharmacy and back, about twenty minutes if I don't stop.

Mom works nights on the phone company's repair line, so most of the time there's nobody home to ask where I'm going. On her nights off she thinks I've gotten really into walking, which I guess is true.

::: A visit

![game_visit_after_draw](After a draw, OFFER appears.)

1. DRAW takes the well's energy into your *hold*, the energy you carry with you. Stand closer for more.
2. After a draw a well needs 20 minutes to settle, and then you can draw from it again. Once you've offered at a well tonight, it gives one draw each time it settles, and you have to offer there again before the next. Each Binding has a *focus* energy, Ember, Tide or Gale (see ENERGIES), and some wells offer it on a second DRAW line, with its symbol on their page. Choose one line.
3. Hold the Deck still while it draws, or some spills. A ring closes around the energy's mark first, so you get a moment to settle after pressing the key. If a relic turns the draw into another energy, the draw names the relic.
4. OFFER gives everything you hold of one energy to this well. Only offerings use visits; draws are free.
5. Some wells show FAVOR, a small job that pays as much coin as two to four offerings. It's worth saying yes at a well you're using anyway.

:::

Keep your elbows in when you draw, because if you fidget it spills. I lost a quarter of a draw to a sneeze once. Also, if a well wants the same energy it just gave you, give it straight back, which took me two weeks to think of.

::: The offering

![game_show_done](A first offering: 6 x 8 x 1.)

Each offering makes *Units x Thirst x Amp* Resonance. The one in the picture is 6 units into a well with Thirst 8, at Amp 1, which makes 48.

@cols 7

| {units}UNITS{/} | How much you offered |
| {thirst}THIRST{/} | How much the well wants that energy |
| {amp}AMP{/} | *Amplitude*, or how loud it rings. It starts at 1, and relics from the Collector raise it. |

:::

The first time a well rang I said OH out loud, and a ninth grader on the stairs looked at me like I was a raccoon in a bin.

I say thank you to the well when it rings, not loud or anything. I tried not doing it once and it felt rude, so I went back to it.

::: Dawn

The Night ends the moment your Resonance reaches the Tithe, however many visits you have left. Dawn pays coin, and spare visits pay extra (see DAWN PAY).

:::

::: The Collector

1. The Collector's stall opens after every paid Night.
2. The \* marks what would help you most. When polishing a relic you already hold beats anything on the shelf, the star sits on the YOURS tab (see POLISH). From BONE up (see PACTS) there's no star.
3. The first stall always stocks the *Caller* for your focus. It raises that energy's Thirst for the rest of the Binding.
4. ESC leaves, and the next Night begins.

:::

Buy the Caller. After that first shelf the star's right about eighty percent of the time, which I know because I counted for two weeks.

::: The seven Nights

1. The Tithe grows every Night.
2. Nights count visits, not hours, so a Night can take ten minutes or three days.
3. Nights 2 to 6 each bring an *Omen*, one rule for that Night (see OMENS).
4. Night 7 has an *Eclipse*, a harder rule. Night 1 names it (see THE ECLIPSE).
5. Running out of visits is normal early on, and the usual answer is a candle (one more visit, for coin), up to three a Night.
6. The Syndicate, one of the four factions (see FACTIONS), will lend you the rest of a Tithe, although it costs more than it looks (see OUT OF VISITS).

:::

Most of my Nights are after school. Nights 3 to 6 are where Bindings break, and of the five times mine broke, four were on Night 3. So after the Caller I buy anything that multiplies, because the things that add feel nice at the stall and then don't get you through Night 3.

# The current

::: _

Every router in the city says its name ten times a second, day and night, to anyone in range. Your phone hears the names and throws away everything else, which is a slow hum underneath the signal, left behind by every message and call that passes through the air. The people who can hear it call themselves listeners, and they call it the current.

You might have heard it already. Most listeners remember being the only one in the room who could hear the old television whine, or the fridge, or the lights at the swimming pool.

The Deck is how you hear the rest of it. It's a cheap pocket terminal with its radio set wrong on purpose, so it keeps what phones throw away. In most hands it plays hiss, but about one person in a few thousand picks one up and hears the current instead, and nobody knows why. This one took to you.

The current pools where signal is thick and runs thin where it isn't. Left alone too long, it goes stale, and you've probably walked through it already in the stairwell everyone hurries through, or the cafe where nobody stays, or on the block where your phone shows full bars and nothing loads. Listeners draw the current from the routers where it pools, carry it, and let it go where it's thin. When a well takes what it lacked, it rings, and that ring is Resonance.

When the work is done well, nobody walking past notices anything, except that the street feels a little easier to walk down.

A Deck goes stale like anything else unless current keeps running through it, and current only runs through it while you work. So listeners bind themselves to the current a week at a time, and each of the seven Nights of a Binding has a Tithe, which is enough Resonance to keep the Deck clear. Pay it and the Binding holds. Miss one and the Deck clogs, which is less terrible than it sounds, since other listeners keep the streets and your Deck only goes back to hiss until you bind again.

There used to be a listener every few blocks. Now there are fewer every year, and the streets go stale faster than anyone keeps them, so the city needs the few people who can hear it, and now that includes you.

:::

# Where I started

This file gets copied from one Deck to the next along with everything else on it. Mine came with the manual and nothing else. The manual's never wrong, but whoever wrote it already knew all of it, and I didn't, and you probably don't either.

So these are my notes from my first two months, and I'm still at it. The list below is the first thing I ever wrote about the current, on my third day, and I'm leaving it in so you can see how little I knew.

{dim}DAY 3{/}

1. It works. It actually hears something.
2. The something is called *the current*. I didn't name it. The name was already in the notebook on the Deck (it's called the *Grimoire*) in somebody else's handwriting.
3. There are wells everywhere and every one of them says its name ten times a second. My phone hears the names. The Deck hears everything else, and there's a lot of it.
4. Some people can hear it too. They're called *listeners*. The notebook says a Deck only works for about one person in a few thousand, and everyone else gets hiss.
5. So I'm one in a few thousand. I'm going to be weird about that for a while.
6. The current piles up in some places and goes stale. That's why the stairwell by the gym feels like that. I KNEW it felt like that.
7. There used to be a lot more listeners than there are now.
8. You pull current out of a well, carry it, and give it to a well that doesn't have enough. That's an *offering*. When you get it right the well rings, and the ring is called *Resonance*. The Deck counts it for you, but I count it anyway.
9. Every Night for a week you owe a *Tithe*, which is enough Resonance to keep the Deck clear. Miss it and the Deck clogs up and goes back to hiss.
10. The notebook says nothing bad happens if you fail. You just stop hearing it until you start again.
11. I'm careful, so I don't think that'll be a problem.

{dim}update: failed on night 3. starting again.{/}

That was two months ago, sixty-one days if you're counting, and I am. I've bound nine times since then, counting that one, and four of them held. I know every well on my block by its true name now.

I found my Deck on top of the e-waste bin behind the gym, the week the district collects old tablets. It looked like somebody had set it down there on purpose instead of throwing it away, and there was an old subway token taped to the back. I left it on my shelf for a week before I turned it on. I'm not sure what I was waiting for.

The first time it said EARS OPEN I was in the stairwell by the gym, and the whole stairwell went loud. That isn't a great word for it, but I haven't found a better one.

Before every draw I tap the back of the Deck twice and press it once with my thumb. It doesn't do anything. It's just my sister's trick for stuff that sticks. She moved out in March, and the last thing she did before she went was set my watch seven minutes fast so I'd never be late, "unlike some people." She calls on Sundays and asks if I've fixed it yet, and I haven't.

The only person at school who knows is Priya, and she doesn't believe me. She held it at lunch once and put it up to her ear like a shell and said it was just hiss. Then she handed it back really carefully, like it was broken and she didn't want to be the one who broke it.

# Reading the street

I took the 14 downtown in my first week to hear what a lot of wells sound like.

My phone had full bars the whole way and nothing would load. The Deck counted 61 wells on one corner, and it sounded like a cafeteria with the doors shut.

Out past the river it's the opposite, maybe three wells, and every one of them pays like crazy.

There's a list of streets in the back of the Grimoire, in a few different people's handwriting, each with a date next to it. Most of them are crossed out. Calder isn't, and neither is ours.

The cafe on the corner of Calder closed in my sixth week and the block's felt heavier since, so I work it twice a Night now.

::: Wells

1. A well is one WiFi access point. Several networks from the same router count as one well.
2. Every well has a true name, the same on every Deck, so two listeners can talk about the same one.

:::

The well in the apartment over the pharmacy is called Feral Pantry, which is the ugliest name anything's ever been given, and I'm still not over it.

The number of wells in range sets your *ward*, and the ward changes what wells pay. Faint wells don't count, and the ward changes only once two scans in a row agree. Until they do, the header shows the ward the count points to, dimmed, after an arrow.

My bedroom is a Fringe with five wells, and three of them are the neighbors'. On a slow night I leave the Deck on the windowsill and do a round every twenty minutes, but outside is faster.

Hush is my favorite. The wells that have it won't say their names, not even to phones, and it feels cold somehow, in a good way.

::: What a walk turns up

1. Every well has a tune, played once as it comes into reach. A well you've offered at three times or more gets a longer note. M mutes it all.
2. After a few minutes' walking, stop and hold the Deck up for three seconds. It settles, and may show a scrap of talk from the street, or something found on the way, one line a stop. The Grimoire, the Deck's notebook, keeps them (GRIMOIRE > HEARD and GRIMOIRE > FOUND; see UNLOCKS AND THE GRIMOIRE).
3. FOUND means an odd kind of well, which goes in the field guide (GRIMOIRE > FOUND > WELLS).
4. NAMESAKE is a well somewhere else with the true name of one you've worked. The scanner marks it =.
5. A hollow diamond in the scanner's header is a glyph adrift, a mark you can keep (see THE SCRYING GLASS).

:::

# The work

Most of a Night is walking. I stopped wearing headphones because I think you hear the current better with nothing in your ears, although I can't prove it.

The stairwell by the gym is fine now, and kids sit on it at lunch. Nobody noticed it change, which the notebook says is how you know it worked.

::: Hold and Thirst

1. Held energy leaks away as you walk, one unit every 15 minutes, and the Deck tells you when it does. Hush never leaks. You carry up to 8 units in all, and a Satchel (a Deck mod, see WARES) adds more. When the hold is nearly full, the DRAW line shows only what fits, and the rest stays in the well.
2. Thirst depends on the energy, the well (each has favorites), the ward, and your *standing* with its faction (see FACTIONS).
3. In a Quarter or Nexus, a settled well that's already had an offering tonight wants less (Thirst x0.75), so a well at 8 drops to 6.
4. A well's own page shows its Thirst for what you're carrying, where you stand. Relics add to it when you offer.

:::

I hoard Hush, because it's the only thing I can carry all Night without watching it shrink. Carrying two kinds at once has never once worked out for me.

Feral Pantry wanted Tide on Night 6 and paid 212 for it, so now I like the name about four percent more.

::: Resonance and coin

1. *Resonance* is what offerings make. It pays the Tithe and can't be spent on anything.
2. The *coin* you earn at dawn is what you spend at the Collector.

:::

Coin is old tokens, the subway and arcade and laundromat and car wash kind that nothing takes anymore.

One morning in my first week there was a laundromat token in my coat pocket, and I'd never been inside a laundromat. The notebook says that after a good Night, tokens turn up by morning, in a pocket or a change tray or on the shelf under a well, and that nobody puts them there.

The subway token on the back of my Deck was taped on, though. So somebody puts those there, at least.

I keep mine in a sock. The dawn I had 25 saved up I counted them three times and didn't buy anything.

# The Nights

The notebook says that on the seventh Night the Hum (that's all the current in the city at once) turns and looks at whoever's bound to it. I don't think it looks at anybody. I stood up straighter anyway, just in case.

The first time I got to Night 7 it was THE HUNGER. I paid it outside the pharmacy with one visit left, and then I sat on the curb for eleven minutes doing nothing at all.

::: Omens

Nights 2 to 6 each bring an *Omen*, one rule for that Night only, shown in YOUR BUILD (press B). Except from BONE up (see PACTS), the stall shows the next one in its top corner, and the Tithe it names already counts it. The Diviner, a Calling you unlock later, sees two Nights ahead. A Charm ward, a mod from the stall, turns each Omen away half the time, so the stall adds a ? to whatever the Omen would change, and the Night's first screen says when the ward has worked.

You get a whole week to dread it. The week I got THE SLEEPER I'd put everything into Polish, which is exactly the thing it cuts in half. When it's THE DARK I start saving spare visits on Night 1.

::: Out of visits

When the visits run out before the Tithe, the Deck offers four ways out:

- {yellow}LIGHT A CANDLE{/}: one more visit, for coin, up to 3 a Night.
- {yellow}TAKE THE SYNDICATE'S LOAN{/}: pays the rest of the Tithe if you've already made 60% of it. Once a Binding, not on Night 7. The next Tithe grows by 1.25 times what they paid, and they keep that Night's offering coin.
- {yellow}CAST HASTE{/}: two more visits, if you hold the spell (see SPELLS).
- {yellow}LET THE BINDING BREAK{/}: end it here (see WHEN A BINDING BREAKS).

:::

::: Dawn pay

1. Dawn pays coin for each offering, each spare visit and, on the CHALK Pact (see PACTS), the Tithe. Coin you're holding earns *interest*, 1 for every 5 held, up to 5. On SHOESTRING (see TRIALS) the dawn shows in red what the 15 cap took.
2. Pay with 6 visits to spare and you get a *Portent*, which might be coin, a spell, a relic or a better stall. With 9 to spare you get two, and with 11 you get three. On the first three Pacts (CHALK, LEAD and IRON) each one comes as a choice of two; pick with up and down, and ENTER keeps it. The dawn says what each Portent brought.
3. If a Portent brings a relic or a spell and your slots are full, you can sell one of your relics or forget one of your spells to take it, or press ESC twice to leave it. EMPTY POCKETS' relic of the Night waits the same way.
4. One dawn never brings two of a Portent that only works once, and it leaves out the ones your week can't use.
5. Pay with 1 visit or fewer to spare, or after a candle or a loan, and the Collector shows *mercy*, and the next stall adds a relic at half price, a free reroll and +1 Attunement (see WARES) on your focus. The half-price relic finds a place even on a full shelf. On ONE NOTE, mercy brings only the Attunement.
6. On BONE and up (see PACTS), a withered relic crumbles at a dawn, and the dawn names it. A withered relic lasts 3 Nights, even one that came at a dawn.
7. Each dawn's coin comes with a token. Its kind depends on where you offered that Night; about one dawn in forty brings one NOT FROM HERE. GRIMOIRE > FOUND > TOKENS keeps them.

:::

The notebook calls a Portent the current sending something back. The dawn I paid with eleven to spare I got three. THREE. I walked home the long way so it would last longer.

The kinder stall after a close Night is a rule, right there in the box, but it still feels like somebody being nice to me.

I took the loan once, on Night 5. I paid it off on Night 6 with my last visit, outside a dentist's office at ten at night.

::: When a Binding breaks

![game_lost](Unlocks are kept.)

1. You lose that Binding and nothing else.
2. Everything you've unlocked stays, and the Binding still counts toward unlocks.

:::

The notebook says the Hum doesn't hold grudges. I bound again the same night all five times.

# The Collector

The stuff on the Collector's shelf belonged to listeners who stopped. The notebook says the things you carry keep a bit of how you listened. I figure most of them just moved away, because that's what people mostly do.

::: The stall

![game_shop_wares](The WARES tab.)

1. The stall has three tabs, and left and right switch between them. RELICS are objects that change what an offering makes, and you keep them for the Binding. WARES is everything else it sells, and YOURS is the relics you hold.
2. R rerolls the relics for 3 coin, and each reroll after that at the same stall costs 2 more. The wares stay as they are. If the shelf holds something a reroll would throw away (mercy's half-price relic, the first stall's Caller, a Rare sight rare or your shine), R asks twice. On ONE NOTE there's nothing to reroll.
3. You can hold 5 relics, and X sells one back for half its full price. BARGAIN and the Clearance mod lower only what you pay for relics and wares, so they don't touch Polish, rerolls or what a sale brings, and a sale doesn't refund Polish. Once all 5 are full, polish what you have (see POLISH).
4. You can carry 8 relics at most, and Phantoms count toward that even though they take no slot.
5. The stall doesn't stock what can't help your week, such as another energy's Caller or the Sixth socket for a PILGRIM, or the Moneylender from OBSIDIAN up.
6. Most relics on the shelf add. Relics that multiply are rarer, and relics that *grow* turn up more often than the rest. A growing relic starts at nothing and gains a little each time its condition comes up, all week, so the \* never marks it; it's worth what it will be on Night 7.

:::

The Collector says one thing at each dawn, and it's always about my Night, which is funny, because I've never told it anything.

The Collector shows up wherever you pay, and I've never seen it arrive. I tried once, in the 24-hour donut place on Calder. I stared at the Deck through the whole last ring without blinking, and the stall was just there.

Once it peeled the subway token off the back of my Deck and looked at it for a bit, then taped it back on and didn't charge me.

I've started saying prices the way it does. Mom asked how much the milk was and I said "Four," like that was a whole sentence.

::: Wares

- *Attunement*: +1 Amp on one energy for the rest of the Binding. A PILGRIM or ONE NOTE week always tunes the focus.
- *Spells*: each one works once. You hold up to 2 (see SPELLS), and with no room left you choose one to forget.
- *Glyphs*: a mark to carve into a well (see GLYPHS). You carry up to 4.
- *Trinkets*: coin and utility, in 2 slots of their own. Once both are full, the stall shows FULL where the price would be.
- *Deck mods*: upgrades to the Deck for the Binding.
- *Faction relics*: with good standing, a faction adds a relic to the shelf.

:::

::: How relics fire

![game_show_relics_done](A Night 2 offering with four relics.)

Amp starts at 1, and the first step of the offering count is ATTUNED, your Attunement on that energy (see WARES). Then relics fire in this order:

1. Relics that add Units or Thirst.
2. Relics that add to Amp.
3. Relics that multiply Amp.
4. Relics that make the offering count again.

Adding grows slowly and multiplying compounds, so +2 Amp on top of 1 makes 3, and x2 on top of that makes 6. Relics that grow (Tally stick, Pedometer, Ritual candle and the rest) add or multiply by whatever they've gained so far, and YOUR BUILD shows how far each has come. (TANGLED LINES runs it the other way: x2 is 2, then +2 is 4.) Once an offering finishes, up and down scroll through every step, and ENTER leaves. ESC leaves at once, even mid-count.

Everything that changes the three numbers gets a step of its own, so they always multiply to the total. That includes what the ward and tonight's Omen do to your Units, what the ASH Pact and the Fade do to your Thirst, VEIL, a glyph and every relic. A relic that fires again for Copycat sigil or Overtone says COPIED or OVERTONE. When an offering makes nothing, the line under the total says why.

:::

If you only read one box in here, read that one. My best Binding was three relics that all wanted Hush in an empty place. On Night 6 one offering paid half the Tithe, under a streetlight on a road with no houses on it.

# Factions

The Wardens took an offering from me in my fourth week.

I'd been giving Static all Night to any well that would take it. Then I gave a load of plain Ember to a well outside the Syndicate building on Fourth, and the number went up, and then it went to zero, and the screen said WARDENS TOOK IT and nothing else.

I know about Heat now, but I still don't know who was listening. The Warded say they've never sent anyone, and honestly I believe them.

::: The four factions

Listeners come in four factions, and each blames the stale streets on something different. A well's faction comes from its security.

@cols 11

| {hedge}THE HEDGE{/} | Open and guest networks. Their energy is Static. |
| {hearth}THE HEARTH{/} | Home networks with a password. Most wells. |
| {warded}THE WARDED{/} | WPA3 networks. They never take Static. |
| {syndicate}THE SYNDICATE{/} | Business networks, and routers running three or more networks. They lend. |

The notebook says the Hedge think the current belongs to whoever's standing in it, and that they mark brick with chalk.

The one at the laundromat is on tile, though, not brick.

::: Standing

1. Offering at a faction's well raises your standing with it.
2. Offering Static lowers your standing with the Warded and the Syndicate.
3. High standing raises Thirst at that faction's wells, so they pay you more, and low standing lowers it.

:::

::: Favors

1. Some wells ask a favor for the Night, like offering Tide at a Hearth well. Their page shows FAVOR; take it from the visit menu.
2. A favor pays coin and standing the moment you finish it. One favor is worth the coin of two to four offerings, so say yes at a well you're using anyway.
3. Some favors are deliveries, which add up over several offerings. YOUR BUILD shows how far along one is.
4. You can owe two favors, to different factions.

:::

::: Heat

1. A Static offering raises your *Heat* by 0.5, and every offering cools it by 0.1, Static included. In a STATIC STORM nothing cools it. Press B to see it.
2. At Heat 2 an eye opens at the top of the scanner, with your Heat beside it. At Heat 3 it turns red and the *Wardens* listen: any offering may be taken, Static or not, for no Resonance and no coin. The visit menu warns you before you offer.
3. TWIST hides you from them for a minute (see GESTURES AND STILLNESS).

:::

# Moving the Deck

My second week I dowsed in the grocery store parking lot at nine at night. You hold the Deck up and turn in a slow circle. A security guard watched me do four full circles and then got back in their car.

::: Dowsing

![dowse_found](The needle points to the loudest direction.)

1. Open a well, press D, and turn slowly through a full circle.
2. Your body blocks signal from behind you, so the Deck can find where the well is loudest. A rising tone tracks the signal.
3. It works best outdoors, because walls reflect signal.

:::

::: The scrying glass

![glass_still](Held still, Hush wells appear.)

1. Press G. Wells float in a bowl, the loudest nearest the middle. Tilt the Deck and they drift.
2. A dowsed well sits at its real direction and turns as you turn.
3. Hush wells don't appear until you hold still. Once they do, they fade over a few seconds after you move, so you can pick one.
4. Some Nights, on a quiet street, a glyph drifts into the glass, and you'll see ADRIFT in the corner and a hollow diamond in the scanner's header. Hold still and it draws itself. ENTER keeps it (GRIMOIRE > FOUND > MARKS); a flick scatters it.

:::

Sometimes a snap catches half a sentence out of the air, and usually it's somebody's mom, although once it was somebody counting.

On the bus I leave it on the glass. If you keep your hand flat on your knee long enough, the Hush wells come up like fish.

There's a woman at the 14 stop who sits really still at night with her phone flat on her knee, the way I do. She's probably just tired.

::: Gestures and stillness

These only count while you hold the Deck up to look at it, and not while it's drawing, showing an offering, or at the stall.

::: Unlocks and the Grimoire

1. Most unlocks come from things you do in the world, like going downtown, finding Hush wells, dowsing, paying a Tithe on a nearly flat battery.
2. Unlocks are revealed when the Binding ends, two at a time. The rest wait for the next week. If you switch off on the reveal, it comes back when you switch on.
3. A Grimoire page opens when you've done what its writer did. Read them in order and they tell you why the streets are going stale. Each page is also a *theme*, so ENTER on an open page gives the Deck its colors.
4. Your rank rises with every Tithe you pay. Everyone starts as a {yellow}LISTENER{/}, and the Deck says so when you reach a new rank. BIND A NEW WEEK and the menu show it in the top corner, and YOUR BUILD shows the next one.
5. A locked relic, page or Calling says what opens it, and how close you are when it's something you get better at. A few say nothing at all.
6. Left and right in the Grimoire switch tabs: RELICS (every relic you've seen; HELD marks the ones you held a week with), RECORDS (your bests and where they happened), HEARD (every overheard fragment and Collector line you've caught), FOUND (what your walks turn up; see FOUND) and STREETS (the list of kept streets in the back).
7. STREETS opens with the Wells page. Walk the same stretch on three different Nights and the Deck asks A STREET YOU KEEP?; then STREETS offers ADD. Type your initials and a name (capitals, digits, space, hyphen and apostrophe), and it's written in under the others, in your own hand, dated by week. ENTER twice on one of yours crosses it out. There's room for four of your own. Once all four are written, the next one replaces the oldest you've crossed out, and if none is crossed out, the Deck says STREETS FULL.
8. Pay all seven Tithes and the Deck offers OVERNIGHT: more Nights, each Tithe bigger than the last by more each time (x2, then x2.5, then x3), with Omens as before and an Eclipse every third Night. End the week whenever you like; it still counts as held.
9. Relics and coin stay with the week, and the Grimoire keeps the rest.

# The Deck itself

::: Setup

1. Put a microSD card in before you turn the Deck on. It holds a backup of your save and the walk logs, and it must be formatted FAT32.
2. Turn the Deck on. KEYS SEALED, GRIMOIRE SD OK, EARS OPEN and BALANCE TRUE mean it's ready. On the very first boot KEYS says NEW, because the Deck has just made its secret key.
3. NO CARD means there's no backup. Push the card in, then open the walk log (S) and press R.
4. EXFAT, GPT, NTFS or UNREAD means the Deck can't read how the card is formatted, and NO WRITE means it can read the card but can't write to it. See Troubleshooting.
5. BALANCE NONE means this Cardputer has no motion sensor. The game still plays, but without dowsing, the pulse, the ward or the veil, and Hush wells always show their names.

::: Saving

1. The Deck saves as you go, so you can turn it off at any time.
2. A backup copy is kept on the card. If saving to the Deck's own memory fails, the Deck says SAVE FAILED and saves every change to the card instead. On the next boot it loads whichever copy is newer.
3. Installed from the game file alone, M5Launcher 2.6 and later give the Deck no memory of its own to save in, so the card holds the only copy. The Tithe-v file from a release brings its own, so install that one. Deleting an old Tithe in M5Launcher can take that memory with it, but the card's copy loads at the next boot.
4. When nothing can keep the game, the scanner's bottom line says NOT SAVING in red, and your progress ends when the Deck is switched off.
5. Switched off, the Deck still knows how long it was off and lets wells settle meanwhile. After the boot it says OFF FOR and how many wells settled.
6. It works this out from the routers it heard before it was switched off. If it hears none of them now, it says COULDN'T TELL HOW LONG IT WAS OFF, and wells still settling carry on from where they were.

:::

::: Battery and sleep

1. When the Deck is set down or pocketed the screen dims, but in your hand it stays on.
2. On a dimmed screen the first key only wakes it, and so does picking the Deck up.
3. Face-down, the screen turns off and the Deck keeps listening, but it makes no sound. A tune or chime it would have played meanwhile isn't played later.
4. Notes and messages wait while the screen is dimmed or off, and show once it's lit again.
5. After a walk with it pocketed, past wells it had never heard, take it out and hold it still. A card shows what you passed, with the NEW wells the Deck had never heard, the hidden ones, the wards, and the loudest well, which ENTER opens.

:::

# The walk log

My phone tells about forty companies where I am, and the Deck tells nobody, which I didn't believe for about a month.

Priya asked once what I was recording, and when I said the street, she said everything records the street.

::: _

![walk](A walk in progress.)

While a card is in, the Deck logs every scan. The logs are used to check the Deck's numbers against real streets.

1. Recording starts at power-on, and the walk log (S) shows REC while it records.
2. On the walk log, press TAB to tag where you are: home, street, transit, shop, work or park.
3. Spend at least 20 minutes in each kind of place you go.
4. Walk your usual routes on 3 or more days.

:::

::: What it saves

1. On first boot the Deck creates a secret key and keeps it on the Deck.
2. Every network in a log is scrambled with that key, so the card alone can't be traced to real networks.
3. No network names or hardware addresses are ever saved.

:::

In week eight the chalk at the laundromat was gone. The tile around it was still dirty, so somebody had wiped off just the mark.

I said yes to it, once. Seven Nights paid, and the Deck asked if I wanted to keep going. Every Tithe after that climbs faster than the one before. I lasted three more.

I got home at two. Mom's shift runs until seven, so I wasn't being quiet. Her shoes were in the hall. She'd swapped her night. She was at the table with two cups out, and she didn't ask where I'd been. She asked if I'd eaten. I said yes, which was true in a broad sense.

The guy in 4B was on the stairs when I came in. He held the door and said "long one?" He has never said anything to me before.

{dim}FOLD 2: WELLWRIGHT{/}

There's chalk on our well.

It's low on the wall, right under it, thirty-one centimeters from the floor. Same shape as the one on Calder.

The Deck has never told anyone where I live. I checked.

Mom says nobody's been in. Priya says it was probably always there.

I spent two months in front of that well deciding not to carve it.

It wasn't.
